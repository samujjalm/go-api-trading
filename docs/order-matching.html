The matching rules

Every incoming order (the taker) is matched against resting orders on the other side (the makers). These rules are enforced in internal/domain/matching.go, and the simulator above applies the same ones.

Best price first

A buy matches the lowest ask first; a sell matches the highest bid first. Matching walks outward level by level for as long as the price still crosses the taker's limit.

BUY 140 → 189.50 first, then 190.00

Then first come, first served

At the same price, the order that arrived earliest fills first. The service orders by an arrival sequence (seq) rather than a timestamp, because timestamps can tie.

190.00: Carol #3 fills before Alice #5

The resting order sets the price

Trades execute at the maker's price. A buyer willing to pay 190.50 who hits an ask at 189.50 pays 189.50, and the reserved difference goes back to their available cash.

bid 190.50 × 30, fills @ 189.50 → $30.00 released

Market orders never rest

A market order takes whatever liquidity exists and cancels any remainder (immediate-or-cancel). A market buy reserves a cash budget and stops when it can't afford one more share.

$25,000 budget → 100 @ 189.50 + 31 @ 190.00

Limit remainders join the queue

Whatever a limit order can't fill rests on the book at its limit price, behind every older order at that price.

SELL 90 @ 189.00 → 80 fill, 10 rest as new best ask

No trading with yourself

If the next maker belongs to the same user, matching stops and the rest of the new order is cancelled ("cancel newest"). That prevents wash trades and keeps the book from crossing.

Alice BUY @ 190.00 vs Alice's ask → cancelled

One order through the service

What happens, in order, when POST /api/v1/orders arrives. The highlighted steps run inside one database transaction, so the order, every trade and every balance change commit together or not at all.

inside the transaction
  1. Decode and validateJSON is decoded strictly, then domain.NewOrder checks symbol, side, type, price and quantity bounds.
    adapters/http/trading_handlers.go
    domain/order.go · NewOrder
  2. Begin transactionRetried automatically if Postgres aborts it for a deadlock, so the body must be safe to run twice.
    adapters/postgres/store.go · WithTx
  3. Lock the book for this symbolpg_advisory_xact_lock makes orders for the same symbol match one at a time. Other symbols proceed in parallel.
    adapters/postgres/orders.go · LockBook
  4. Idempotency checkIf this user already sent the same client_order_id, return the original order instead of trading twice.
    app/trading_service.go · PlaceOrder
  5. Reserve fundsCash for buys, shares for sells, moved from available to reserved. Not enough means 422 insufficient_funds and everything rolls back.
    domain/balance.go · Reserve
  6. Fetch match candidatesOpen orders on the other side that cross the limit, best price then lowest seq, 100 at a time.
    adapters/postgres/orders.go · MatchCandidates
  7. MatchThe pure function that applies the rules above and produces trades. No I/O, so it is unit-tested with plain tables.
    domain/matching.go · Match
  8. Settle each tradeFour balances move: buyer pays cash and gets shares, seller gives shares and gets cash. Every change is written to the journal.
    domain/balance.go · Settle
  9. Finish the takerFilled, rests on the book, or cancelled (market / self-trade). Any leftover reservation is released.
    domain/order.go · FinishMatching
  10. CommitThe book lock is released and the next order for this symbol can match.
    adapters/postgres/store.go
  11. Respond201 {order, trades}, or 200 with Idempotent-Replayed: true for a retry.
    adapters/http/trading_handlers.go

Where the book actually lives

The ladder above is drawn from memory, but the service keeps no in-memory book. The book is the set of open rows in the orders table: bids are open BUY rows, asks are open SELL rows.

A partial index covers only open orders, sorted by symbol, side, price and arrival sequence. That makes "best ask for AAPL" an index lookup no matter how many filled orders pile up.

This trades speed for simplicity. Each order costs a few milliseconds of database round trips, where an exchange's in-memory engine takes microseconds. In exchange, the database is always the source of truth and crash recovery needs no extra work.

-- 0001_init.sql: index over resting orders only
CREATE INDEX orders_book_idx
  ON orders (symbol, side, price, seq)
  WHERE status IN ('NEW', 'PARTIALLY_FILLED');

-- MatchCandidates for an incoming BUY
SELECT ... FROM orders
 WHERE symbol = $1 AND side = 'SELL'
   AND status IN ('NEW', 'PARTIALLY_FILLED')
   AND ($2::bigint IS NULL OR price <= $2)
 ORDER BY price ASC, seq ASC   -- price, then time
 LIMIT 100 FOR UPDATE;
Runs entirely in your browser; nothing is sent to the API. The simulator applies the service's matching and reservation rules to a fixed starting book. Balances other than a market buy's budget are not modelled here; the service checks them before matching.
